Heading to a gaming convention, comic show, or Pokémon-focused event with stacks of cards? Perfect. Shows are one of the best places to move cards you no longer need while picking up merch, sealed product, or other collectibles you actually want. The key is selective: keep your high-value chase cards and graded slabs safe at home (or locked in a display case if you just want to show them off). Bring the rest—the solid, liquidatable stuff that dealers actively buy and trade.

Here’s a practical guide to turning extra cards into convention hauls without regret.

### Why Trade Instead of Sell Outright?
Dealers at shows are there to buy, sell, and flip. Many prefer trades because it keeps inventory moving and cash in their pockets for bigger deals. You can often get better effective value in merchandise (boosters, boxes, playmats, figures, apparel, or other TCG product) than pure cash, especially if the dealer is motivated to clear specific stock. It also feels less like “selling out” of your collection and more like upgrading.

### What Cards to Bring (and What to Leave Home)
**Leave at home or locked up:**
- Your absolute top chase cards, especially anything graded PSA/BGS/CGC 9+ or high-end modern hits (special illustration rares, full arts, etc. that have strong long-term demand).
- Sentimentally important cards or ones you’re still hunting sets for.
- Anything you’re not emotionally ready to part with. FOMO hits hard when you see a dealer flip something you just traded.

**Bring these instead:**
- Bulk commons/uncommons and solid rares from recent sets (Scarlet & Violet era, older modern). Dealers buy bulk by the thousand for resealing or grading potential.
- Mid-tier holos, reverse holos, and playable competitive cards (trainers, energy, popular attackers).
- Complete or near-complete sets of lower-value eras.
- Older base-set era or WOTC cards that aren’t the absolute heavy hitters but still have demand.
- Japanese or foreign language cards if they’re clean—some dealers specialize.
- Sealed product you no longer want (blisters, ETBs, collections) can also work in trades.

Aim for volume + liquidity. A couple hundred decent cards or a few binders of sorted modern product usually move better than a handful of random high-end singles you’re attached to. Organize everything: sorted by set or type in binders or toploaders so dealers can browse quickly. Time is money on a busy show floor.

### How to Approach Dealers
1. **Scout first.** Walk the floor and note which dealers buy Pokémon aggressively (look for “We Buy Cards” signs, big cash displays, or stacks of sorted product). Note their specialty—some want modern playables, others vintage, others bulk.

2. **Be direct and polite.** “Hey, I’ve got some modern bulk and mid-tier holos I’m looking to trade for [specific merch—boosters, a box, playmats, etc.]. Interested in looking?” Dealers appreciate people who know what they want.

3. **Know rough values.** Check recent sold listings (eBay sold, TCGPlayer market) beforehand so you have a baseline. Expect dealer buy prices around 40–70% of market depending on condition, demand, and how easy the cards are to move. Trades can sometimes stretch that a bit if they’re unloading slow-moving merch.

4. **Negotiate the trade.** Offer a pile, ask what they’d give in product. Be ready to walk if the offer feels low—there are usually multiple buyers on the floor. Bundle deals often work better than single cards.

5. **Inspect what you’re getting.** Check sealed product for damage, verify authenticity on higher-end items, and confirm quantities.

### Pro Tips for a Smooth Experience
- **Cash backup helps.** Some dealers prefer a mix of cards + a little cash to balance the trade.
- **Bring a small scale or just trust volume counts** for bulk—many dealers buy by weight or rough count.
- **Condition matters.** Clean cards in sleeves or toploaders get better offers. Dirty or heavily played modern cards move slower.
- **Taxes and fees.** Some shows take a cut or have vendor rules; factor that in.
- **Security.** Keep your trade binder on you or in a secure bag. Shows get crowded.
- **Have fun with it.** Part of the appeal is the hunt—finding a dealer who needs exactly what you have and has the exact ETB or figure you’ve been eyeing.

Trading extras at shows is one of the most efficient ways to refresh your collection without dumping cash. You walk in with cards collecting dust and walk out with new product you’ll actually open or display. Just be disciplined about what stays in the “never trade” pile. Those keepers are the ones that make the rest of the collection meaningful.

Safe travels, and may your binders be full and your trade offers fair. See you on the floor.
