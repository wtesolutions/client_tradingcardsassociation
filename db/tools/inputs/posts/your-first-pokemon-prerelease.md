Last updated: October 2026

A Pokémon prerelease is the friendliest “big event” most shops run. You sit at a table with people who also like opening new cards. You build a small deck from a kit the shop provides. You play a few short matches. You go home with cardboard you did not have that morning. No one expects you to be a tournament regular.

This weekend is a particularly good time to try one. Official Pokémon press says Play! Pokémon prerelease events for **Mega Evolution—Delta Reign** begin October 24, 2026 at participating independent retailers. The expansion itself arrives at stores on November 6, 2026. If you have been waiting for a reason to walk into a shop instead of refreshing a marketplace listing, this is that reason.

## What a prerelease is (and is not)

A prerelease is an early-play event for a new Pokémon TCG set. Shops that are part of the Play! Pokémon program can run them in the window before street date. You register, you receive a kit tied to the new set, you build a deck on the spot, and you play in a casual structured format.

It is not a high-stakes championship. It is not a silent auction for sealed cases. It is not a promise that you will pull a specific card. Think of it as a guided first date with a set: enough structure that shy players have something to do, enough chaos that everyone opens something different.

Details vary by store. Entry price, round count, whether you keep every card you open, and whether energy or extra packs are provided are shop decisions. NTCA will not invent a national price or a prize table. Call the store or read their event listing. Official Play! Pokémon pages and the Pokémon Event Locator are the right place for sanctioned-event specifics; your local shop is the right place for “do I need to bring sleeves?”

## How to find a table this week

Start with two lists, not one.

**Official sanctioned events.** Use Pokémon’s Event Locator (linked from Pokémon’s own prerelease coverage) to see Play! Pokémon stores running Delta Reign prereleases. That list is about tournament registration, player IDs, and the program’s format.

**Independent shops you actually want to visit.** Use the [NTCA shop directory](/shops). Filter for a store near you, then phone or email and ask: “Are you running Delta Reign prereleases starting October 24, and do you still have seats?” Some shops run several flights over the weekend. Some sell out. Some are not Play! Pokémon locations but still have a learning night or a casual open-packs table—ask; do not assume.

If you are a parent, ask two extra questions: is the flight all-ages, and is there space for a grown-up to sit nearby without playing? If you are a shop owner reading this, put those answers on your social post. First-timers will come if they know they are allowed to be first-timers.

## What the day usually feels like

Arrive a little early. Bring a photo ID if the shop asked for one, a player ID if you already have a Play! Pokémon account, and a small pile of supplies: water, a pencil, and sleeves if you own them. If you do not own sleeves, buy them at the counter before round one. That purchase is not upselling. It is how your new cards survive the car ride home.

Check-in is usually a name on a list and a kit on the table. Official Pokémon coverage for this prerelease describes a Build & Battle-style experience—cards from the new set plus a way to build a smaller-than-usual deck so matches stay short. Confirm the exact kit and format with the shop the day you register. Contents and house rules can differ, and we will not recite a packing list that the publisher or the store might adjust.

Then you sort. This is the part new players love and overthink. Put Pokémon in one pile, Trainer cards in another, Energy where you can see it. Ask the person next to you what they are trying to do. Prerelease tables are one of the last places in the hobby where “I have no idea” is a normal sentence.

Matches are short on purpose. You will win some and lose some. The point is to touch the new cards before November 6, learn a couple of attack names, and decide whether this set is a player set, a collector set, or both for you.

When you leave, sleeve the cards you care about before you hit the parking lot. If the shop has a weekly Pokémon night, put it in your phone.

## A first-timer packing list

- **A plan for payment and pickup.** Some shops want you registered and paid in advance. Some hold a kit at the counter. Do not assume a walk-up seat on a popular Saturday.
- **Sleeves, or money for sleeves.** New cards scuff fast on a shop table.
- **Time, not just an hour.** Between check-in, deck building, and a few rounds, this is an afternoon activity.
- **A parent or friend if you are new.** Playing is more fun than hovering, but hovering is allowed. Shops would rather have a comfortable first family than an empty chair.
- **Realistic luck.** You are there to play and to start a set, not to finance a vacation. If a table talker starts quoting “what this will be worth,” smile and keep sorting.

Shop owners: print a one-page “first prerelease” sheet. Where to sit. Where the restrooms are. Whether food is okay at the table. How pairings work. That sheet does more for the hobby than any poster of a chase card.

## After October 24, keep the habit

Delta Reign’s street date is November 6, 2026. Prerelease weekend is the preview. The shop is still there on November 7. If the event went well, come back for singles, a starter product you actually want to open, or the next learn-to-play night.

NTCA’s job is to make that return trip easy. Browse the [shop directory](/shops), scan the [show calendar](/calendar) if you would rather hunt a convention floor, and keep [New to Trading Cards](/new-to-trading-cards) handy for condition language before you buy your second stack. See you at a table.
